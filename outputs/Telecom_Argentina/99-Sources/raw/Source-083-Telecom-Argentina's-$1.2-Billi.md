Telecom Argentina's $1.2 Billion TMA Deal Drives Revenue, Faces $1.8 Trillion Financial Reversal
Aug 13, 2025
·
Aug 12, 2025
TEO
Summary
Telecom Argentina, a dominant force in Argentina's telecommunications sector, recently navigated a significant strategic shift in the first half of 2025. The period was marked by the transformative acquisition of Telefónica Móviles Argentina (TMA) and a notable swing in financial performance, highlighting the complexities of operating within a hyperinflationary economy.
On February 24, 2025, Telecom finalized the acquisition of TMA for a contractual purchase price of US$1.245 billion. This strategic move, primarily funded by new loans totaling US$1.17 billion, aims to bolster existing services and expand network coverage and capacity across Argentina. TMA’s consolidation notably impacted Telecom's results, contributing $958 billion to the overall $3.36 trillion in revenues, an increase of 44% compared to the first half of 2024. Despite this revenue surge, the financial statements for 2025 are explicitly not comparable to prior periods due to TMA’s integration.
The most striking financial development was a profound shift from a net income of $1.2 trillion in the first half of 2024 to a net loss of $75.5 billion in the first half of 2025. This nearly $1.8 trillion reversal was predominantly driven by a significant change in financial results from borrowings, which swung from a $1.56 trillion gain in the prior year to a $221 billion loss. This dramatic shift primarily reflects foreign currency exchange differences on borrowings, a volatile factor in Argentina's hyperinflationary environment where the US dollar appreciated 17% against the Argentine peso during the period.
Operationally, Telecom Argentina continued to invest heavily in its core infrastructure. Capital expenditures increased 54% to $481 billion in the first half of 2025, directed towards expanding 4G/LTE and 5G networks, alongside deploying fiber optics to enhance broadband services. TMA contributed $132 billion to this investment. The company also demonstrated strong performance in its digital offerings; the Personal Pay digital wallet saw its user base jump 41% to 4.2 million, showcasing successful diversification into fintech services.
Telecom actively managed its debt profile, issuing US$800 million in Series 24 Notes in May 2025 and subsequently using the proceeds to prepay US$784 million of loans acquired for the TMA deal. Despite these efforts, total borrowings increased to $4.4 trillion. While the company maintains a negative working capital of $1.84 trillion—a typical structure for capital-intensive telecommunication firms that benefit from spontaneous supplier financing—its liquidity rate improved to 0.43, and it remains compliant with all debt covenants.
However, the TMA acquisition faces ongoing scrutiny from Argentine regulatory authorities, including a preliminary objection report from the Argentine Antitrust Commission (CNDC). While Telecom is challenging these preliminary conclusions and has proposed potential remedies, the outcome remains uncertain. The company emphasizes its commitment to upholding competitive conditions and believes its strategic arguments are robust. As Telecom continues to integrate TMA and pursue its investment roadmap, its ability to navigate the complex regulatory landscape and macroeconomic conditions will be key.
