Overview
1.1        Please briefly describe the: (a) telecoms, including internet; and (b) audio-visual media distribution sectors in your jurisdiction, in particular by reference to each sector’s: (i) annual revenue; and (ii) 3–5 most significant market participants.
The telecommunications sector, including internet services, is a significant economic contributor in Argentina.  In 2020, the top four companies leading in revenue reached a combined total of nearly 10 billion dollars annually and are among the 25 highest-grossing companies in the country.  Telecom, Telefónica and Claro hold the top positions.
[i]
The sector benefits from substantial demand for high-speed internet, largely driven by the growing need for mobile data and broadband connectivity across the country.  Within the telecommunications industry, mobile communications hold the largest market share, representing 44% of total revenue, followed by subscription TV services at 22%, fixed internet at 18%, fixed-line telephony at 10% and audiovisual communication services at 6%.
Argentina’s telecommunications market is dominated by a few large national and multinational companies, alongside smaller regional providers.  Among the primary players are Telecom Argentina (Grupo Clarín), a comprehensive provider offering fixed-line, mobile, internet and pay-TV services; Claro (América Móvil), which holds extensive market coverage across mobile and internet sectors; and Movistar (Telefónica Group), known for its wide-reaching infrastructure and competitive services in both mobile and internet.  These leading companies generate a substantial portion of the sector’s revenue, ranking among the top 25 highest-grossing companies in the country.  They cater to a diverse range of clients, including private businesses, government entities and households.
In the audiovisual media distribution sector, annual revenue is approximately 1.5 billion dollars, primarily from television broadcasting, streaming platforms and content production.  Major players in this sector include Grupo Clarín, a dominant force in free-to-air television through Canal 13 and in cable via: Cablevisión Flow; Telefónica Group (Movistar TV), which is active in both pay-TV and streaming; and DirecTV, a significant provider of satellite television services with extensive reach across the region.
Both the telecommunications and audiovisual media sectors in Argentina are regulated by ENACOM (National Communications Entity).  ENACOM is responsible for overseeing compliance, supporting infrastructure development, and enforcing industry standards to ensure sustained growth and high-quality services across these essential sectors.
1.2        List the most important legislation that applies to the: (a) telecoms, including internet; and (b) audio-visual media distribution sectors in your jurisdiction, and list any other significant legislation on the horizon such as the regulation of online harms, regulation of social media or artificial intelligence (but only so far as they will likely impact such industries).
The provision of Information and Communications Technologies (ICT) Services in Argentina is primarily governed by Argentina Digital Law No. 27,078 (ADL).  This law establishes the regulatory framework for ICT services, aiming to promote universal access, fair competition and the integration of digital technologies across Argentina.  The ADL sets forth definitions, general rules and market standards for a broad spectrum of services, including telecommunications, internet services and audiovisual communication.
On the other hand, the provision of broadcasting services in Argentina is mainly governed by Audiovisual Communication Services Law No. 26,522 (ACS Law).  The ACS Law establishes that audiovisual communications services are an activity of public interest and includes a set of definitions, general rules and market standards.  It also covers rights and obligations for audiovisual communications services providers, regulations for advertising agencies, content producers and television channels.
Additionally, a robust consumer protection regime in the audiovisual and telecommunications sectors is vital for ensuring a fair and transparent market, where consumers are treated equitably.
