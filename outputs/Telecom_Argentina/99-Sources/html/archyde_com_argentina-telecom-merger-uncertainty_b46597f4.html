Buenos Aires, Argentina – The Argentinian government is once again expressing reservations regarding Telecom Argentina’s aspiring plan to acquire Telefónica Móviles Argentina. This proposed $1.25 billion acquisition has ignited fierce debate about the future of competition within the nation’s telecommunications landscape.

The core concern revolves around the potential emergence of a duopoly, a scenario that could stifle innovation and harm consumer interests. What measures will be taken to ensure a competitive market in this evolving situation?

Regulatory Hurdles for Telecom Argentina

Following a thorough evaluation by The national Commission For The Defence Of Competition (CNDC), The Secretariat Of Industry And Commerce (SIyC) has formally objected to the proposed merger. Regulators fear that allowing Telecom Argentina to assume complete control of Telefónica’s Argentinian operations would substantially undermine fair competition within the telecom sector.

telefónica, operating under the Movistar and Tuenti brands, delivers a extensive suite of services, including mobile and fixed telephony, broadband internet, and pay-TV. Telecom Argentina is a major player in fixed and mobile telephony, high-speed internet, and cable television.

Competition Concerns and Market Dominance

One of CNDC’s primary concerns is that this acquisition would eliminate one of Argentina’s three major mobile phone operators, effectively creating a two-player market. Under this scenario,Telecom Argentina would control approximately 58% of the market,while AMX (Claro) would hold the remaining 42%. Moreover,the merger would push telecom Argentina beyond the legally permissible spectrum cap of 140 MHz in several key regions.

Further complicating matters, the merged entity could dominate the internet market in 114 specific areas, exceeding a 40% market share.similarly, fixed-line telephone services could see near-monopolies in 143 areas. These potential imbalances have triggered serious regulatory scrutiny.

Impact on Corporate Telecommunications and Bundled Services

The CNDC also anticipates that the combined entity’s market share in corporate telecommunications services,exceeding 50%,could negatively impact competition within this strategic segment. Bundled service offerings could also suffer, possibly leaving Telecom as the only provider capable of delivering nationwide “quad-play” packages, encompassing mobile and fixed telephony, internet, and pay-TV.

Did You Know? Quad-play services are increasingly popular, offering convenience and potential cost savings for consumers. Though, a lack of competition could lead to higher prices and reduced service quality.

Mitigation Measures and Future Hearings

These concerns are not new.The US$1.25 billion deal, initially agreed upon earlier this year, was previously placed on hold by the Argentinian government. The next step involves a special hearing designed to explore potential measures to mitigate the merger’s potential adverse effects on market competition.

Reportedly,Telecom Argentina has been granted fifteen days to propose solutions that address the risks identified by the CNDC,to be presented at this crucial hearing. How will Telecom Argentina address these significant regulatory concerns?
