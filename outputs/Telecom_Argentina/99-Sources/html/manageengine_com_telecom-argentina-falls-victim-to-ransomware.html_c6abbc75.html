A leading ISP company in Argentina became victim of a ransomware attack on July 18. This attack is one of the biggest cyberattacks in Argentina's history. The malicious actors demanded ISP company Telecom Argentina pay a ransom of $7.5 million to retrieve the encrypted files.

The attack happened over the weekend, and it was identified that the ransomware actors managed to gain control over an internal Domain Admin account, from which they distributed the malware payload to 18,000 workstations.

Although the hack was successful, the attack did not affect the ISP's internet connectivity to customers; however, the company's official websites were down for a while.

How did Telecom Argentina respond?

The company detected the attack at an early stage and restricted employees from interacting with the corporate network, connecting via internal VPN, and opening any spam emails or their attachments.

Who was behind the hack?

The REvil ransomware group, the same group responsible for Sodinokibi ransomware, has claimed responsibility for the attack. The group hosted a webpage that displayed the ransom demand in Monero, a form of cryptocurrency, which is around $7.53 million; they've also mentioned that if the payment isn't made in three days, the ransom amount could be doubled.

REvil gang has already been involved in a number of incidents, wherein they usually infiltrate a network through unpatched networking equipment or applications and spread laterally throughout the company's network. They've already breached Pulse Secure, Citrix VPN, and Srilanka Telecom in the past.

Additionally, Telecom Argentina was running a vulnerable Citirx instance, CVE-2019-19781, for a month even though a patch was made available, said Bad Packets, a threat intelligence company.

Like most rasnomware attacks, REvil will disclose the data to the dark web if the ransom is not paid in time.

How Telecom Argentina could have avoided REvil

Based on the details discussed above, the attackers breached into the network through a spam email, then exploited an unpatched Citrix vulnerability, and finally took control of the Domain Admin account. Although the company reacted quickly by detecting the breach and educating its employees of cyber best practices, the chaos could have been avoided altogether if the company had acted proactively.

  • Avoid malicious attachments by telling employees to stay away from anonymous and suspicious emails.
  • Fix network-level vulnerabilities as soon as patches are available.
  • Patch all endpoints and their applications in a timely manner.
  • Scrutinize the open ports to avoid lateral infections.

Telecom Argentina is a wonderful case study on why being proactive is always better than reactive. When it comes to ransomware threats, it's essential to employ proactive cybersecurity for your enterprise, even if you're taking full backups regularly.
