    FX Volatility Trumps Operational Mastery at Telecom Argentina as Net Debt Surges 44%

    Nov 10, 2025
    TEO

    Summary

    Telecom Argentina’s 9M25 results reveal a profound disconnect between outstanding operational performance and overwhelming financial volatility, defined by the hyper-inflationary Argentine economy.

    The company reported a consolidated net loss of P$272.5 billion, a figure entirely driven by an adverse foreign exchange environment. Specifically, a P$2.6 trillion higher real loss from foreign exchange differences severely impacted net earnings. This massive financial outflow occurred because the Argentine Peso’s 33.7% depreciation against the US dollar significantly exceeded the 22.0% inflation rate during the nine-month period.

    In stark contrast to the financial headline, operational efficiency in the core business (excluding TMA) achieved exceptional results. Management successfully enacted a 3.3% real decrease in operating costs, which drove a 4.2 percentage point expansion in the Operating Income before D, A & I margin, reaching 33.0%. This cost deflation, achieved in a highly unstable economic climate, demonstrates superior operational discipline.

    Driving the core profitability was the company’s sustained pricing power in its mobile segment. Mobile service revenue grew 4.1% in real terms, underscored by a robust 13.6% real increase in mobile average monthly revenue per customer (ARPU). This ability to aggressively price services and keep pace with 31.8% accumulated inflation is paramount for real-term value preservation and is supported by ongoing network quality improvements, such as increasing 4G speeds from 66 Mbps to 85 Mbps.

    The financial risk is amplified by a surge in leverage. Consolidated Net Financial Debt increased 44.3% in constant currency to P$4.4 trillion since December 31, 2024, primarily funding the TMA acquisition. This enlarged debt profile heightens the company's future exposure to adverse FX and inflation mismatches, potentially compromising long-term capital stability.

    Telecom Argentina, a major Argentine telecommunications and media services provider, is navigating this environment by seeking scale and diversification. The TMA acquisition offers strategic opportunities, particularly leveraging TMA’s higher real ARPU growth in broadband (+10.3%) and TV (+19.5%) for potential pricing harmonization across the merged subscriber base. However, the integration faces hurdles, including a 48.5% rise in consolidated labor costs and high churn in TMA’s Pay TV services, which saw a 6.9% subscriber decline and a 4.0% average monthly churn rate. Simultaneously, the company is successfully diversifying into high-growth, non-regulated fintech, evidenced by Personal Pay’s expansion from 3.3 million to 4.4 million users, driving 20.6% real growth in ancillary services. This diversification acts as a hedge against the persistent 16.3% real-term decline observed in fixed voice and data revenues, a segment struggling structurally to keep pace with inflation.

    Key Facts

    • Exceptional Real Cost Reduction and Margin Expansion: Telecom (excluding TMA) achieved a 3.3% real decrease in operating costs, driving a 4.2 percentage point expansion in its Operating Income before D, A & I margin to 33.0% in 9M25. This real cost deflation in a hyper-inflationary economy proves superior operational efficiency and pricing discipline.
    • Sustained Real Service Revenue Growth and Mobile Pricing Power: Telecom (excluding TMA) achieved 4.1% real growth in service revenue, primarily driven by a robust 13.6% real increase in mobile average monthly revenue per customer (ARPU). This sustained ability to outpace 31.8% inflation is paramount for real-term value preservation.
    • Critical Deterioration of Financial Results due to FX: The P$272.5 billion consolidated net loss resulted overwhelmingly from a P$2.6 trillion higher real loss from foreign exchange differences. This massive swing occurred because the Argentine Peso's 33.7% depreciation against the US dollar significantly exceeded the 22.0% inflation rate in 9M25, severely impacting net earnings.
    • Significant Increase in Constant Currency Net Debt: Consolidated Net Financial Debt surged 44.3% in constant currency since December 31, 2024, to P$4.4 trillion, primarily driven by financing for the TMA acquisition. This enlarged debt profile heightens the company's future exposure to adverse FX/inflation mismatches.

    Red Flags

    • Structural FX/Inflation Mismatch Risk: The P$685 billion net financial loss in 9M25, driven by a P$2.6 trillion real loss on foreign exchange differences, highlights a structural vulnerability where the currency depreciates faster than local inflation. The recent 44.3% constant currency increase in net debt amplifies this high-impact, non-cash financial risk, potentially compromising long-term capital stability.
    • Rapid Decline in Core Fixed Telephony Pricing Power: Fixed voice and data revenues for the legacy Telecom business declined 16.3% in real terms because pricing failed to keep pace with the 31.8% accumulated inflation. This suggests severe regulatory or competitive limitations on pricing in a core legacy asset, causing sustained real-term value erosion in the segment.
    • High Customer Churn in Acquired Pay TV Segment: TMA’s Pay TV services saw a 6.9% decline in subscribers, exhibiting a 4.0% average monthly churn rate, which is significantly higher than Telecom's legacy rate of 1.5%. This high churn, despite achieving 19.5% real ARPU growth for TMA TV, indicates that aggressive pricing is potentially undermining the subscriber base and complicating long-term synergy realization in this segment.

    Strategic Implications

    • Refocus on Mobile and Broadband Margin Health: The successful attainment of 13.6% real mobile ARPU growth and a 3.3% real decrease in core operating costs confirms the strategy of prioritizing margin health and pricing power over volume. This strategy is reinforced by the voluntary disconnection of 5.0% of mobile lines with no traffic, optimizing the customer base for efficiency.
    • TMA Integration for Scale and Potential ARPU Harmonization: The TMA acquisition successfully added scale, contributing significantly to the consolidated results. TMA's higher real ARPU growth in broadband (+10.3%) and TV (+19.5%) signals a key strategic opportunity to leverage the combined entity for upward pricing harmonization across the merged subscriber base, using M&A to combat chronic inflation.
    • Validating Fintech as a Core Diversification Strategy: The 20.6% real growth in ancillary services, driven by the expansion of the Personal Pay digital wallet (users grew from 3.3 million to 4.4 million), validates the strategic move into high-growth, non-regulated fintech. This diversification provides a valuable, high-margin hedge against the challenging pricing environment of core telecom services.

    Key Positives

    • Powerful Margin Expansion in Core Business: Telecom (excluding TMA) expanded its Operating Income before D, A & I margin by 4.2 percentage points to 33.0%, while simultaneously achieving a 3.3% real reduction in operating costs. This is an exceptional operational achievement, demonstrating management's mastery of efficiency in an unstable economic climate.
    • Strong Mobile Pricing Power: Mobile service ARPU for the core Telecom business increased 13.6% in real terms. This aggressive and effective pricing strategy is the bedrock of profitability, enabling the core business to outgrow the 31.8% Y/Y inflation rate in its most vital segment.
    • Successful Fiber and Network Quality Deployment: Accesses with speeds of 100 Mbps or higher now represent 97% of the total broadband subscriber base, up from 87% in 9M24. The concurrent increase in 4G speeds (from 66 Mbps to 85 Mbps) justifies premium pricing and fortifies the company's competitive moat against rivals.

    Key Negatives

    • Massive Real Loss from Currency Volatility: The P$272.5 billion consolidated net loss was entirely driven by the P$2.6 trillion higher real loss on foreign exchange differences. This massive financial volatility, tied directly to the 44.3% real increase in debt, means net earnings are fundamentally uncoupled from strong operating performance.
    • Aggressive Constant Currency Debt Growth: Net Financial Debt increased 44.3% in constant currency to P$4.4 trillion to fund the TMA acquisition. This aggressive debt load increases systemic risk and may constrain future capital allocation flexibility, prioritizing debt management over necessary growth CAPEX.
    • Fixed Telephony Segment Cannot Keep Pace with Inflation: Fixed voice and data revenues (excluding TMA) declined 16.3% in real terms. This persistent pricing weakness in a legacy segment indicates a sustained erosion of real value, suggesting structural resistance to price increases that may extend to other regulated services.
