Carvana's Growth and Financial Performance

Author Sheldon Kuphal

Carvana's Growth and Financial Performance was nothing short of remarkable. The company's revenue grew from $1.4 billion in 2017 to $6.2 billion in 2020, a staggering increase of 342%.

Carvana's growth was fueled by its innovative business model, which allowed customers to purchase and finance vehicles online with a 7-day return policy. This approach helped the company expand its market share and reach new customers.

In 2020, Carvana's gross profit margin was 13.6%, a significant improvement from 7.1% in 2017. This increase in profitability was largely due to the company's ability to reduce costs and increase efficiency in its operations.

Carvana's financial performance was also boosted by its acquisition of DriveTime in 2020, which added 130 locations to its network and expanded its customer base. This strategic move helped the company further establish itself as a leading player in the used car market.

Business Operations

Carvana Co. operates its e-commerce platform for buying and selling used cars in the United States. The company offers a range of services including vehicle acquisition, inspection and reconditioning, and post-sale customer support.

The company was founded in 2012 and is based in Tempe, Arizona, where it has established a presence as a major player in the used car market.

Challenges and Feedback

Carvana has a reputation for having numerous customer complaints. Communication problems are a recurring issue, with customers reporting getting the runaround on paperwork, fees, and claims.

Many customers have reported getting blindsided by Carvana's internal financing rates, which can be higher than those of other lenders. This can be a significant issue for customers who may not be aware of the higher rates until it's too late.

Vehicle condition issues are another theme, with customers reporting receiving vehicles with undisclosed damage and mechanical issues. One buyer even reported receiving a vehicle with bald tires and misaligned wheels, despite Carvana's 150-point inspection.

Warranty and repair coverage have also been a source of frustration for customers, with some reporting slow service and high costs. In some cases, the issues have gone beyond poor customer service, with Carvana facing accusations of deceptive practices.

Customers have reported random delays when trying to get their car delivered, with no clear explanation provided by the company. Some customers have even reported receiving vehicles with significant issues, such as hail damage or a blown head gasket, that were not disclosed to them.

Carvana's seven-day "love it or return it" policy is intended to alleviate these issues, but it's unclear how effective it is in practice. The company's response to customer complaints has been inconsistent, with some customers reporting difficulty in getting help when they need it.
